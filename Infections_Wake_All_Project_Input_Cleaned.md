## 1. Initial Game Concept

The game is called **Infection’s Wake**.

The main gameplay is that you start in a forest, and then you have to find rocks on the ground because you do not have tools. Then you craft them into tools with sticks that you can get from branches on trees. Then you can make an axe or something, chop down a tree, use rocks to make firewood, and make a fire. Then you can cook food after you kill an animal.

At night, zombies come in waves, and your sanity lowers. At 50%, zombies spawn during the day. At 0%, harder zombies spawn.

During the daytime, your sanity goes up, and during the night, it goes down because you are stressed.

To progress, you have to find ore mines, and you can start using machines to automate mining. You will find different ores, and there will be bosses that you have to defeat to get more advanced ores and special items.

It should be a side-scroller sandbox like *Terraria*.

You can ask a few questions about the game, like five, and then we will answer them.

## 2. Main Goal, Scoring, Death, World, and Automation

The main goal of the game should be to defeat a final boss and to try to survive as long as possible.

You get points for surviving a day, and you get points from defeating zombies. You are trying to get as many points as possible.

When the player dies, they return to a spawn point if they have made one. If they have not made one, they lose all progress.

They have to make a spawn point out of something. If they do not make one, they restart completely. If they do make one, they lose their entire inventory and return to the spawn point.

The world will open up with forests, caves, and plains. There could be abandoned cities and laboratories, except there should mainly be caves with blocks of ore in them. You can put a drill on them, and then it will mine them.

Players can build and automate. They can make a base with walls, traps, turrets, farms, and mining machines. There should be some way to have electricity, kind of like in *Factorio*.

They have to defend against increasingly difficult zombie waves.

There should be a level indicator on the side of the screen that says what level the wave is and when it is coming, like a meter.

When it is nighttime, it should say something like, “Next wave in three minutes.” Then the wave comes. After the wave, you wait a minute or two, and then it says, “Next wave in three minutes.”

There should be a wave about every five minutes, and you have to defeat those waves.

## 3. Final Boss Access, Sanity Recovery, Persistence, Enemy Scaling, and Multiplayer

The player should reach the final boss through some kind of dungeon.

Once the player gets some items from ruins, they can repair some kind of machine. Then it opens a door, and there is a boss battle.

Sleeping in a bed could also restore sanity.

Lights should make sanity restore a little faster.

Everything in the world should remain exactly the same after death except that the player does not have the items that were in their inventory.

The player’s score, defeated bosses, buildings, machines, and tunnels should all remain.

Zombie waves should become harder by having more zombies and stronger zombie types.

There should be enemies that climb walls, enemies that damage machines, and occasional boss waves.

All zombies should damage machines. Once they get into the base, it should be kind of like *Factorio*: they eat your machines, and then they eat you.

The game will be single-player only for now. Multiplayer might be incorporated later.

---

## 4. Spawn Machine, Inventory Recovery, Technology Tiers, Post-Boss Play, and Low Sanity

The spawn point is not a bed, special shelter, or medical station.

It is a machine that needs to be powered, and it has to require some kind of special item. Otherwise, it would be too easy to get.

Lost inventory should remain as a gravestone or dead body, kind of like in *Terraria*. Then you can go back and get the items.

The main technology tiers should be:

1. Stone tools
2. Iron equipment
3. Steel equipment
4. Steel and electricity
5. Turrets and powered machinery
6. Advanced technology

Once the player reaches steel, electricity should become available.

After defeating the final boss, the player should keep going because they can always upgrade their base more.

It should be kind of like *Minecraft*: you beat the Ender Dragon, and then you keep going.

The game should not become increasingly difficult forever after the final boss. It should stay at the hardest tier.

If the player has beaten the final boss, they should be able to defeat the hardest zombies.

Low sanity should cause zombies to spawn during the day.

At around 25% sanity, false enemies should appear. They should flicker, and if the player hits one once, it disappears. They are basically ghost enemies.

At 0% sanity, the hardest zombies should be able to appear, like the ones that normally appear after the player beats the final boss.

# Consolidated Current Design Decisions

The following section collects the latest user decisions in one place. It does not replace the chronological input above.

## Game Format

- Title: **Infection’s Wake**
- Single-player for the initial version
- 3D voxel sandbox world
- Influences include *Minecraft*, *Vintage Story*, *Terraria*, and *Factorio*
- Multiplayer may be considered later

## Starting Gameplay

- Start in a forest with no tools
- Gather loose rocks and sticks
- Craft primitive tools
- Chop trees
- Make firewood and a fire
- Kill animals and cook food

## World

- Forests
- Plains
- Caves
- Ore blocks or deposits
- Ruins
- Abandoned cities
- Laboratories
- A final dungeon or sealed laboratory area

## Progression

- Stone tools
- Iron equipment
- Steel equipment
- Electricity
- Turrets and powered machines
- Advanced technology
- Repair a machine using items from ruins
- Open the final dungeon
- Defeat the final boss
- Continue playing afterward

## Mining and Automation

- Place drills on ore deposits
- Automate mining
- Build farms
- Build traps
- Build turrets
- Build electrical systems
- Defend machinery from zombies

## Waves

- Nighttime zombie waves
- Roughly five minutes between wave cycles
- A side-screen wave meter
- Wave level
- Countdown to next wave
- Increasing numbers of zombies
- Stronger zombie types
- Wall-climbing enemies
- Machine-damaging enemies
- Occasional boss waves
- Maximum fixed difficulty after the final boss rather than endless scaling

## Sanity

- Sanity rises during the day
- Sanity falls during the night
- Sleeping restores sanity
- Lights improve sanity restoration
- Sanity is entirely negative, not tactical
- Below 50%, zombies may spawn during the day
- Around 25%, false enemies appear
- False enemies flicker and disappear after one hit
- Low sanity may cause false generator alarms, misread heat traces, and phantom movement
- At 0%, the hardest enemy types may appear
- At 0%, there is no continuous automatic health loss

## Death and Respawning

- The respawn point is a powered machine
- It requires a rare or special component
- It is not a bed, shelter, or medical station
- It must be powered when the player dies
- If power fails at the moment of death, the player does not respawn there
- Without a valid spawn point, the player loses all progress and restarts
- With a valid spawn point, the player respawns there
- Carried inventory is dropped at a body or gravestone
- The player can return and recover it
- The world, score, defeated bosses, buildings, machines, and tunnels remain

## Main Goal and Scoring

- Defeat a final boss
- Survive as long as possible
- Earn points for surviving days
- Earn points for killing zombies
- Continue increasing the score after the final boss

## Lore Direction

- Biologically based infection
- Bacteria or germ-like origin
- Similar in broad concept to Project Lazarus
- No hive mind
- No central intelligence
- No single controlling creature
- No machine consciousness
- Zombie behavior comes from independent biological infection and mutation
- The exact lore should be included at concept level in the main human-readable specification

## 23. Huge World Update (Verbal Feedback, August 2026)

Feedback on wishlist item 11 after playing the 192×192 build:

> 192×192 is really small; I can go from one end to the other in like 30
> seconds or so. It needs to be a huge world, more like 1024×1024 or
> 10k×10k or maybe even potentially infinite or close to it; and it must
> perform properly, so some kind of virtual loading, etc. — but with
> consistent mechanics through it all. It really needs to feel like a
> world, not a weird biosphere room.

Implemented as a 16,384×16,384-block streamed world: the hand-authored story
valley stays at 0..192 and never unloads; deterministic wilderness (terrain,
caves, forests, nests, finite ore hills) generates chunk-by-chunk around the
player in every direction, out to a containment rim of mountains at the edge
of the region.
